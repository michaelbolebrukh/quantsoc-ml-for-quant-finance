# Workshop 5: can a neural network read a stock chart?

A presenter-led notebook replicating Jiang, Kelly and Xiu (2023), "(Re-)Imag(in)ing Price Trends" (Journal of
Finance 78(6), 3193-3249; free to read at https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13268, working paper at
https://ssrn.com/abstract=3756587), on today's S&P 100: drawing price charts the paper's way, a convolutional
neural network built to the paper's exact specification, out-of-sample accuracy and decile portfolios from 2008,
Grad-CAM heatmaps, a comparison with Workshops 3 and 4, and an Alpaca paper-trading dashboard.

## How the cache was made, and the choices that differ from the paper

* Universe: today's S&P 100 (102 tickers), not every US stock. Expect weaker results: the paper's equal-weight
  portfolios include thousands of small stocks, and its value-weight Sharpe ratios (about 0.5 at the monthly
  horizon, Table 4) are the closer yardstick for a mega-cap universe.
* Training: 2000 to 2007, random 70/30 split for training and validation, early stopping, five networks per
  model, never retrained. Only pictures whose label is known by the end of 2007 are used.
* Learning rate: the paper's 1e-5 learned too slowly with our far smaller training set (validation loss still
  above a coin flip's after two epochs), so the cache uses the rate that did best on 2000 to 2007 validation
  loss in pilot runs (1e-3; the pilots are listed in `build_cache.py`). No test-period number was looked at to choose it.
* 20-day pictures: one training picture every 5 trading days per stock, to fit the CPU this was built on.
* Costs of 5 bps per dollar traded, as in Workshops 3 and 4 (the paper deducts no trading costs).

## Paper-trading dashboard

A Streamlit page that runs the 5-day picture strategy (I5R5) from `quantsoc_ml/cnn.py` on today's prices: it
draws each stock's last 5 completed days, scores them with the five cached networks, and builds a book long the
top decile and short the bottom decile ($0.50 each side per $1 of equity, equal weights). It shows the decile
table, the actual 32x15 charts of the strongest calls, the target book, your positions and the orders that would
move the account there. **Paper trading only**: an offline mock broker or your Alpaca PAPER account.

### Install and run

```bash
pip install streamlit alpaca-py python-dotenv torch
cp .env.example .env          # paste your Alpaca PAPER keys after APCA_API_KEY_ID= and APCA_API_SECRET_KEY=
python -m streamlit run workshop5/dashboard.py
```

`.env` is ignored by git. The dashboard only ever shows *where* it found the keys, never the keys themselves.

### The weekly rule

The strategy trades once every 5 trading days. The page finds your last rebalance from the account itself: the
newest filled order whose client order id starts `qsml-ws5-`. Five or more completed trading days later the
rebalance is due and orders are proposed. Before that the page shows the ranking and book but proposes nothing,
and says how many days remain; a tick box overrides this. With no filled `qsml-ws5` order yet, the first rebalance
is due. Run one strategy per paper account: positions placed by hand or by Workshops 3 and 4 are read as this
strategy's book.

### The two modes (sidebar)

* **Offline demo** (the default without keys): the committed snapshot and a mock broker holding the book the
  strategy chose 5 trading days earlier, so a rebalance is due. Nothing leaves your computer.
* **Alpaca paper**: about 30 trading days of daily bars (open, high, low, close, volume, adjusted for splits and
  dividends) and your account. Today's bar is used only after the close.

Alpaca's free data plan serves bars from the IEX exchange only. Their volume is a small slice of all trading and
their high-low ranges can be narrower than the consolidated Yahoo data the networks were trained on. Each chart
rescales its own volume, but live charts can still differ a little from anything seen in training.

### Safety

Orders are sent only after you tick the confirmation box **and** type `SUBMIT`, and only the plan on screen. The
page refuses if the account changed since the plan was drawn, if any strategy stock has an open order, if the
plan is a hold, or if the new exposure exceeds your buying power (the smaller of overnight and Reg T). Shorts
that are not easy to borrow are skipped and the next stock in line takes the slot.
