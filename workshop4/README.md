# Workshop 4: statistical arbitrage with stochastic processes

A presenter-led notebook replicating Avellaneda and Lee (2010), "Statistical arbitrage in the US equities market"
(Quantitative Finance 10(7), 761-782; free working paper at https://math.nyu.edu/~avellane/AvellanedaLeeStatArb071008.pdf),
on today's S&P 100 from 2001: PCA and sector-ETF factors, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck residuals, s-scores, the paper's
trading rules, a comparison with Workshop 3's ML strategy, meta-labelling, and an Alpaca paper-trading dashboard.

## Paper-trading dashboard

A Streamlit page that runs the Avellaneda and Lee (2010) strategy from `quantsoc_ml/avlee.py` on today's
prices. Each stock's last 60 daily returns are regressed on its sector SPDR ETF, the leftover is fitted as a
mean-reverting process, and the s-score says how far it has strayed. The page shows every stock's s-score,
which trades open, stay open and close today, the target book (stock legs and ETF hedges) and the orders
that would move your account there. **Paper trading only**: it works with an offline mock broker or your
Alpaca PAPER account (practice money), and cannot reach a live account.

### Install

```bash
pip install streamlit alpaca-py python-dotenv
```

### Your Alpaca keys (only for Alpaca paper mode)

1. Log in at https://app.alpaca.markets, switch to **Paper Trading**, and create API keys.
2. In the repo root (the folder with `.env.example`), copy `.env.example` to `.env`.
3. Paste your PAPER keys after `APCA_API_KEY_ID=` and `APCA_API_SECRET_KEY=`.

### The two modes (sidebar)

* **Offline demo** (the default when no keys are found): the committed stock and ETF snapshots and a mock
  broker. The demo replays the strategy from an empty account over 30 trading days and stops on the latest
  day in the snapshot on which trades open, stay open and close, so you see all three. Nothing leaves your
  computer and nothing can be submitted.
* **Alpaca paper**: about 80 trading days of daily bars for the stocks and the hedge ETFs (SPY and the sector
  funds), plus your account, from your Alpaca PAPER account. A bar for today is used only after the close.

Both modes show the account and market clock, a chart of today's s-scores with the bands marked (open a long
below -1.25, open a short above 1.25, close a short below 0.75, close a long above -0.5), the trades opening,
staying open and closing, the target book and the proposed orders. Alpaca paper mode adds your equity
history and recent orders.

### How the page sizes trades

* A trade that opens today is 2.5% of equity in the stock, in whole shares (rounded towards zero). The notebook's
  backtest uses 5%; the page halves it because 40 or so open trades plus their hedges at 5% would need about 3
  times equity, and Alpaca's overnight buying power is about 2 times equity.
* A trade that stays open keeps its current number of shares. That is what the paper does in practice: it
  adjusts the size only for new positions, to save trading costs. The notebook's backtest simplifies by holding
  each open trade at a constant fraction of equity, so live and backtest profit and loss will differ slightly.
* A trade that closes goes to zero shares.
* Each hedge ETF holds minus side times beta times each trade's dollar value at the last close, summed over
  the trades it hedges (SPY when the sector fund lacks history). Hedges are recomputed on every run.
* A held stock with no prices today, or one that has left the universe, is left alone, and so is the hedge ETF
  that covers it (no orders in that fund this run). The page names both.
* A new short that Alpaca says is not easy to borrow is not opened, and the page says so. No other stock is
  used in its place.

### One strategy per account

This strategy remembers nothing between runs: it reads its open trades from the account's **stock**
positions. Long shares in a stock are an open long trade, short shares an open short trade, and ETF positions
are its hedges. So any other stock position in the account, for example from Workshop 3's dashboard or bought
by hand, is read as one of its trades. Use a paper account for this strategy only.

To start again from nothing: on the Alpaca website go to Paper Trading, Positions, and close all positions.
Wait for those orders to fill, then reload the page.

### Safeguards

* Orders are sent only in Alpaca paper mode, only after you tick the box, type SUBMIT and press the button.
  Loading or reloading the page never sends anything.
* The app sends exactly the orders that were on screen when you clicked, and refuses if the account changed
  in between (different positions or open orders, for example after "Close all" on the website). A change in
  equity alone is fine.
* Submit stays off while ANY strategy stock or hedge ETF has an open order, for example a half-filled opening
  order: wait for it to fill, or cancel it on the Alpaca website, then reload.
* Submit stays off when the new positions need more than the account's buying power; the page says how much
  they need. Close some trades first, or use a smaller position size.
* Each order has an id made from the decision date, the symbol and the leg, so pressing twice cannot send it
  twice. One submission per decision date. Alpaca refuses an id it has already seen, so an order that was
  rejected cannot be sent again with the same id on the same decision date: it goes out on the next trading
  day's run instead.
* Exits and reductions are sent first, then the hedge ETF legs that add exposure, then the new stock trades, so
  a new trade is never left without its hedge because buying power ran out. A hedge that flips from long to short (or back) is sent
  as two orders; while the market is closed only the closing one is sent, and the rest on the next run.
* The broker is hard-wired to the Alpaca PAPER endpoint, and error messages are cleaned of your keys before
  they are shown.
* All the trading logic is in `workshop4/live.py`, which reuses Workshop 3's broker layer
  (`workshop3/live.py` and `quantsoc_ml/paper.py`). Tests: `python -m pytest tests/test_ws4_dashboard.py`.

Teaching tool, not investment advice. Market orders fill at a price the page cannot know in advance; the
dollar figures are estimates at the last close.
